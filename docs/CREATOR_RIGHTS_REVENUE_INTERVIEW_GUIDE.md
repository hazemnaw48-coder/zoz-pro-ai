# Interview Guide — Creator Rights & Revenue Kit

## Recruitment profile
English-speaking UGC creators who have completed at least two paid collaborations, delivered at least one asset with explicit usage terms, and handled their own invoice/follow-up workflow. Avoid interviewing only friends or people who already like the product idea. Recruitment/outreach requires owner approval before sending any messages.

## Opening (neutral)
"I'm researching how independent UGC creators manage the terms, deadlines and payment admin attached to brand collaborations. I'm not selling anything in this interview. Could you describe how you handled your most recent paid deal?"

## Questions (ask about actual behavior)
1. Walk me through the most recent deal from inquiry to final payment. What happened first, next and last?
2. Where do you store the agreement or written scope for each deliverable?
3. How do you record which platform, use type (organic/paid), territory, duration and exclusivity were agreed?
4. Who sets the licence start and end dates? Are they tied to delivery, approval, first use, or another trigger according to the agreement?
5. Tell me about the last time a brand asked for extra usage, whitelisting, raw footage, exclusivity or additional revisions. How was the cost decided?
6. Have you ever had to verify whether a public ad was still running? What did you check and where did you save the evidence?
7. Describe the last late invoice or payment follow-up. How did you track the due date and communication?
8. What tools or spreadsheets do you use today? What is each one good/bad at?
9. What has the existing tool or process failed to surface in time? What was the observable consequence?
10. How frequently does this happen, across how many paid deals/assets, and how much time does it take?
11. Have you tried an existing UGC tracker or rights-management SaaS? Which one, and why did you keep or abandon it?
12. If the current process stays the same for six months, what is the likely cost or inconvenience?
13. Which missing capability would make you switch from your current workaround? Ask them to rank: clarity of rights, public-evidence log, expiry workflow, payment chase, quoting, or something else.
14. Would you volunteer to test a spreadsheet that supports that exact workflow? What would it need to include to be worth opening twice?
15. After describing the problem in their own words, test a neutral price-reaction exercise at US$19 and US$29. Ask what they would do at each price and why; do not collect a payment or treat a polite “yes” as a purchase.

## Evidence log fields
- Interview ID (not full name); date; broad segment; recruitment channel; count of paid deals/rights-based assets; actual recent incident; current workaround; consequence/time cost; tools named; unmet gap; prototype willingness; price reaction; follow-up permission.
- Use anonymized notes and quotes only with permission. Do not collect copies of contracts, personal contact details, customer credentials, private DMs, or non-public campaign information.

## Decision thresholds
- Continue to prototype only if at least 8/15 have experienced the same recent rights-scope, licence-window or unpaid-invoice problem, at least 5/15 show a current workaround or consequence, and at least 3 volunteer to test.
- Competitive-gap threshold: at least 5/15 independently identify the same unresolved evidence-to-action need, and at least 3 can explain why existing spreadsheets or software do not solve it.
- Stop or revise if the signal is mainly generic “I like dashboards” interest, existing products are adequate, or interviewees cannot identify a recent, concrete consequence.
